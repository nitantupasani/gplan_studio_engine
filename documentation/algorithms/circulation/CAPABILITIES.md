# Circulation capability matrix

Updated 2026-09-11. The `GPLAN/circulation_engine` registry exposes schema `1.0`, engine `rectangular-boundary-shift` version `1.0.0`, and the four modes below. Production validation and tests determine acceptance. Historical algorithms are inventoried for comparison and are not registered by the new service.

## Shared geometry and input requirements

All new modes operate on existing floorplan geometry with stable room IDs, explicit units, original envelope, entry, coverage, corridor width, allocation policy, room constraints, protected/locked rooms, fixed cores/obstacles and affected opening/asset metadata. They return validated alternatives with adjusted room polygons, explicit wall-free corridor surfaces, per-room area changes, route/run provenance, physical access, metrics, warnings and algorithm/version/settings identifiers. They preserve the base state required for replacement and reject stale-plan application.

The first geometry strategy retains rectangular boundary shifting. It supports axis-aligned room rectangles with complete sides covered by selected contiguous collinear contact groups, including suitable T partitions. It does not require a PTPG. It rejects irregular/notched room inputs, dangling partial contacts, unsupported entries and any combination that cannot meet hard room/geometry/access constraints. An entry must join a supported corridor run physically; a room-center point or abstract adjacency edge is not enough.

All accepted modes validate in-envelope surfaces, locked room/core/obstacle preservation, independent finite-width reachability, doorway-length access to every promised room, exact area accounting, coherent assets and no corridor-owned walls. Boundary shifting additionally enforces actual remaining minimum dimensions/area/aspect. Larger-room contribution is its default; available-area, equal-area and protected-room policies alter allocation independently of route selection. Feasible preference exceptions are reported, not treated as permission to relax constraints. Existing reuse preserves all rooms and their inherited dimensional-rule exceptions unchanged; it takes zero area under every policy and does not recertify the original design.

Existing reuse requires explicit `circulation_room_ids`, `modes: ["existing"]`, `wall_thickness: {interior, exterior}` and the saved `entry.opening_id`. An internal stair portal also requires `entry.stair_room_id` identifying its actual protected/locked owner. Room names do not imply public passage. The clear floor subtracts half the interior wall thickness at shared room edges and full exterior thickness inside the outer-face envelope. Saved openings remain unchanged and must provide the requested doorway width into that clear floor. Reused source polygons may be orthogonal polygons; unchanged ordinary rooms need not be rectangles. Multiple source rooms require a saved fully open shared separator: assigning public IDs does not remove a wall or widen a door.

## Registered public modes

| Mode | Prerequisites and coverage | Objective and exactness | Output guarantee after validation | Runtime and limits | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `existing`: Existing landing/circulation | Explicit existing public source IDs, wall thickness and saved exterior or protected stair portal; directly served all/selected rooms | Validate and reuse the existing clear floor; no route optimization or walking-length claim | All original rooms and openings unchanged; zero contributions; connected clear width and saved doorway access | One deterministic geometry/validation result; shared bounded inputs, no allocation solver or graph search | Cannot create access, widen a narrow landing, erase a separating wall, or pass through undesignated private rooms |
| `spanning`: Spanning circulation | Entry-rooted contact/run graph; all eligible or selected rooms | Deterministic connected breadth-first traversal; no length/area optimum claim | Physical corridor reaches entry and each requested room | Contact construction worst-case O(R²); traversal O(V+E), plus allocation/geometry validation | May use more corridor than a compact network |
| `compact`: Compact circulation | Same graph; all eligible or selected rooms | Incremental shortest attachments plus pruning only when connected coverage remains | Same hard geometry/access guarantees | Bounded repeated routing/pruning plus solver; stop with explicit limit information | No global minimum length, area or contribution objective |
| `shortest`: Shortest route, one selected destination | Fixed positive run-length node costs; one destination | Exact minimum sum of activated full wall-run lengths on the declared fixed graph | A physically validated optimum for those fixed costs, if feasible; otherwise explicit infeasibility | Dijkstra polynomial in graph size, plus allocation/validation; no Yen enumeration in this version | Not arbitrary-polygon Euclidean shortest, shortest final walking distance, or all-room minimum network; rejects infeasible graph optimum without searching substitutes |

Shared ceilings are 64 rooms, 96 wall groups, 512 original openings, a 1 MB request, at most 12 returned candidates (default 6), a 12-second generation deadline, and 160 allocation iterations. The scalar-room bounds and polygon point/holes limits live in `models.py`. The solver is constrained SLSQP with independent hard validation; it has no global nonlinear-optimality guarantee. Return fewer candidates after geometry/topology deduplication. Unsupported mode/coverage combinations return explicit errors rather than silently switching objective. `shortest found` for multiple rooms is researched but **not registered**.

The registered allocation IDs are `area` (default larger-room preference), `surplus`, `equal`, and `protected`. Locked/protected rooms contribute zero under every policy. Public entry points are `generate_circulation(request)` and `validate_circulation({request, candidate})`; validation combines independent geometry checks with deterministic candidate reconstruction to reject changed opening semantics, route metadata, provenance or fingerprints. Input/output units are `ft` or `m`, preserved without implicit engine conversion.

For exact single-destination routing, complete wall-run selection cost and walking distance are different. If selecting a run shrinks its full length, use a precisely declared node/run cost and measure actual resulting geometry separately. When global allocation causes the lowest-cost graph route to fail, exact feasible-route claims require ordered enumeration of all cheaper rejected candidates; a bounded incomplete search can only claim shortest found. [NetworkX shortest paths](https://networkx.org/documentation/stable/reference/algorithms/generated/networkx.algorithms.shortest_paths.generic.shortest_path.html), [ordered loopless alternatives](https://networkx.org/documentation/stable/reference/algorithms/generated/networkx.algorithms.simple_paths.shortest_simple_paths.html)
